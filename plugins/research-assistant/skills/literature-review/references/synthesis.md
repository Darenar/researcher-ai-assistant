# Literature synthesis

Build the review around an argument, not an inventory of papers.

1. State the scope and organizing question.
2. Group studies by concepts, mechanisms, methods, populations, or competing explanations.
3. Within each group, compare findings and the evidence strength behind them.
4. Explain contradictions using design, measurement, context, precision, or bias when the sources support that explanation.
5. Identify what is established, uncertain, disputed, and absent from the local evidence.
6. End with the gap or rationale that follows from the synthesis.

Avoid one paragraph per paper, citation dumping, causal language unsupported by study design, and claims that every retrieved paper agrees. Retain source/page provenance in working notes even when the final citation style omits pages.
